2005

East London / after midnight / peak mess

This middle section can hold more energy. Still readable, still restrained, but slightly denser and more nightlife-driven. You might introduce a pull quote sooner, or interrupt the prose with one sharp visual rhythm change to suggest a city that feels faster and more performative.

Because the whole project is modular, each era should feel like a distinct issue rather than a chapter dropped into the same template. That’s where the portfolio value comes in — you’re showing system thinking, not just formatting text.

“By two in the morning everyone looked like someone they’d promised not to become.”

Keep the extras compact. A playlist, one image link, one line of context. Enough to suggest a wider publishing format, not so much that it starts feeling like an overbuilt app or a moodboard pretending to be fiction.

2015

North-east / cleaner surface / stranger distance

This version should breathe more. More white space. Cleaner typography. A feeling that the interface itself has changed because the world has. You can still keep the emotional mess, but the design language becomes cooler, flatter, and slightly more detached.

For the portfolio framing, this is where your design thinking really lands: the content hasn’t changed medium by accident, it has changed because each era has its own visual and cultural grammar.

“Nothing had got quieter. It had just moved behind glass.”

Once this three-era prototype exists, you can decide whether to expand it into nine issues, keep it as a concept piece, or use it as the basis for a broader digital short fiction platform. But this alone is enough to stand on as a finished, intelligent project.
